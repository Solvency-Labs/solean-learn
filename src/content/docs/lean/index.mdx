Before syntax, the mindset. Formal verification is unfamiliar to most of the team, and the *mental model* matters more than memorizing tactics.

## The core idea

A **formal proof** is an argument written in so much detail that a *computer* can check every step mechanically. A **proof assistant** (we use **Lean 4**) is the program that does that checking. You write definitions and theorems; Lean refuses to accept a "proof" unless it's airtight.

The payoff: when Lean says your theorem is proved, there's no "looks right to me" — it's checked down to the axioms. The risk moves entirely to two places:

1. **Did you state the right theorem?** (Lean checks the proof, not your intent.)
2. **What did you assume?** (Anything you took as an axiom or hypothesis is trusted, not proven.)

This is why the project obsesses over **boundaries** — the value of a proof is exactly as good as your statement and your assumptions. "We proved X **under assumption Y**" is the honest unit of work.

## Modeling, then proving

You can't prove things about Solidity bytes directly. The workflow is always:

```
real artifact   ──model──▶   a precise object in Lean   ──prove──▶   a theorem about that object
(ForsVerifier)               (a function / inductive)                ("recover is correct, given keccak")
```

Two distinct risks live here:

- **Modeling gap** — does the Lean object faithfully match the real contract? (Tools like [EVMYulLean](/solean-learn/tooling/evmyullean/) shrink this gap by modeling the *actual* EVM/Yul, not a hand-wave.)
- **Proof gap** — none, once Lean accepts it. That's the part you get for free.

## Soundness vs. completeness (don't mix these up)

Two words you'll hear constantly, easy to confuse:

- **Soundness** — "if it says *valid*, it really is valid." No false accepts. For our verifier: *you can't make `recover` return the legitimate signer without a genuine signature.* **This is the property that matters for security.**
- **Completeness** — "if it really is valid, it says *valid*." No false rejects. For our verifier: *a genuine signature is always accepted.* This is about not being annoying/broken, not about security.

When in doubt, the scary direction is **soundness**: a soundness bug lets an attacker forge.

## Check yourself

<Question q="When Lean accepts your proof, which risk is eliminated — and which two risks remain?">
The **proof gap** is eliminated: the argument is mechanically checked down to the axioms, so it's not "looks right to me." Two risks remain: (1) **did you state the right theorem?** — Lean checks the proof, not your intent; (2) **what did you assume?** — anything taken as an axiom or hypothesis is trusted, not proven.
</Question>

<Question q="Why does 'we proved X' mean little on its own, without naming the assumptions?">
A theorem is only as strong as its hypotheses. "X" might hold only *under* a strong or even false assumption, in which case the real-world guarantee is weak. The honest unit of work is "we proved X **under assumption Y**" — which is why the project insists on naming every boundary and assumption out loud.
</Question>

<Question q="We treat keccak as an assumption. If keccak were later found broken, what happens to our FORS soundness proof?">
The proof still holds *exactly as stated* — it was always "sound **given** keccak behaves like a good hash." What fails is the real-world guarantee, because the *assumption* failed, not the logic. The theorem is conditional; trust lives in the assumption. That's precisely why we [name keccak as a trusted boundary](/solean-learn/task/) rather than pretending it's proven.
</Question>

## What you do *not* need

You don't need to be a Lean wizard to contribute. Reading definitions, following along in proofs, and writing small lemmas is enough to be useful early. The deep tactic mastery comes with time.
